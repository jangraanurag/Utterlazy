Architecture and Implementation Blueprint: Account Manager Multi-Tenant Scoping and OTP-Gated Approval Workflows
Executive Overview and Ecosystem Context
The evolution of digital aggregator platforms, particularly those tailored for specific geographic and economic demographics such as the Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities targeted by the Khaalo (internally referred to as Welo) ecosystem, demands highly specialized operational roles to bridge the gap between platform administrators and localized vendor partners.1 Operating on a pure aggregator model devoid of direct restaurant ownership, the Khaalo platform requires an architectural paradigm that supports robust localized management while preserving the absolute autonomy, financial sovereignty, and security of the restaurant partners.1 The platform is engineered to rival established giants like Swiggy and Zomato by providing hyper-localized support, which necessitates the introduction of a specialized internal user role: The Account Manager (AM).1
Central to this operational scaling strategy is the deployment of Account Managers. These are internal Khaalo staff members deployed by global platform administrators to manage specialized portfolios of assigned restaurants.1 They provide proactive support in digital marketing, menu curation, campaign management, and overall digital presence optimization. However, delegating promotional capabilities—specifically the creation of financially impactful entities such as Advertisements (Ads) and Promotional Offers (Promo Codes, Buy-One-Get-One deals, and Opt-in Campaigns)—to internal staff introduces significant vectors for financial liability, miscommunication, and unauthorized data mutation.1 If an Account Manager were to inadvertently configure an excessively generous discount or an aggressive Pay-Per-Click budget without the restaurant's consent, the financial damages would be borne directly by the vendor partner, severely fracturing the trust foundational to the aggregator model.
To categorically mitigate these risks, the system architecture mandates the implementation of an impenetrable, cryptographically secure, One-Time Password (OTP)-gated approval state machine.1 Any marketing entity or structurally significant modification created by an Account Manager on behalf of a restaurant enters a suspended lifecycle state and cannot interact with the production catalog, customer-facing applications, or billing engines until explicitly authorized by the restaurant owner.1 This authorization is achieved via an email-based OTP validation executed exclusively within the Flutter-based Restaurant Application.1
This document provides an exhaustive, forensic architectural analysis and implementation blueprint for integrating the Account Manager identity, the comprehensive feature suite available to this role, the multi-tenant scoping mechanisms, the complex state machine configurations, and the strictly regulated OTP approval workflows across the Laravel 13 microservices, the Vue 3 web administration portals, and the Flutter mobile clients.1 Ultimately, this extensive analysis culminates in a highly structured, comprehensive system prompt designed to instruct an OpenCode AI agent in the full-stack development of this ecosystem, satisfying the core developmental directive.
System Architecture and Technological Infrastructure
Before delineating the specific mechanics of the Account Manager workflow, it is imperative to establish the overarching architectural topology of the Khaalo/Welo platform. The system is engineered as a highly decoupled, mobile-primary infrastructure consisting of microservices, WebSocket event broadcasters, and distinct client interfaces optimized for performance and maintainability.1
The outermost layer of the infrastructure is governed by an NGINX reverse proxy, which orchestrates the routing of incoming HTTP and WebSocket traffic across heavily segmented subdomains to ensure distinct operational boundaries.1 The routing configuration directs api.khaalo.in to the core API Gateway, which operates on port :8000.1 This gateway is a Laravel 13 application responsible for critical cross-cutting concerns, including authentication via Laravel Sanctum, centralized Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) dispatch for push notifications, and the hosting of the Filament-powered Vue 3 Admin Panel where the Account Managers operate.1 Conversely, the catalog.khaalo.in subdomain routes to the Catalog Service operating on port :8001.1 This separate Laravel-powered microservice houses the primary business logic, managing over one hundred and thirty endpoints dedicated to the processing of menus, advertisements, and promotional offers.1 Finally, the ws.khaalo.in subdomain targets the WebSocket Server powered by Laravel Reverb on port :8080, which is entirely responsible for real-time, bi-directional event propagation, such as instant updates to the Kitchen Display System (KDS) and the asynchronous delivery of approval queue notifications to the mobile clients.1
Data persistence and rapid retrieval are managed through a highly optimized dual-layer approach. The relational data is housed in a MySQL 8 cluster, strictly partitioned into food_gateway and food_catalog schemas to maintain the integrity of the microservice boundaries and prevent tightly coupled database queries.1 Ephemeral data, rapid state lookups, session management, job queuing, and the critical cryptographic OTP rate-limiting mechanisms are managed via a high-performance Redis 7 instance.1
The client-side architecture is bifurcated into mobile and web domains, adhering strictly to a "Flutter-first" development philosophy where mobile applications are the primary consumption interfaces.1 The mobile ecosystem is structured as a monorepo managed via Melos, comprising shared foundational packages that power three distinct applications: the Customer App, the Restaurant App, and the Rider App.1 These applications leverage the packages/api module for HTTP requests using the Dio client, the packages/core module for robust state management via Riverpod, and the packages/ui module for standardizing design themes and components across the suite.1 The secondary web clients, encompassing the Admin Panel (admin-web), Vendor Portal (vendor-web), and Customer Web, are constructed using modern web architectures featuring Vue 3.5 utilizing the Composition API, Vite 8 for rapid bundling, Tailwind CSS 4 for utility-first styling, and TypeScript for rigorous type safety.1 It is within the admin-web portal that the Account Manager's operational theater resides.

Multi-Tenant Scoping and Pivot Architecture
The realization of the Account Manager role requires a highly sophisticated approach to multi-tenancy and authorization within the Filament Admin framework hosted on the API Gateway.6 Unlike global platform administrators who possess unrestricted, panoptic visibility across the entire food_gateway and food_catalog databases, Account Managers exist within a highly localized, dynamically restricted multi-tenant scope. They must only be able to view, query, and mutate data for the specific subset of restaurants explicitly assigned to them by a higher-level administrator.1
An Account Manager is fundamentally a staff user residing within the standard users table of the food_gateway database. Their distinct privileges are governed by a specific role designation, managed via the Spatie Laravel Permission package or similar Filament-compatible role-based access control (RBAC) integrations such as Filament Shield.6 The critical architectural requirement to facilitate their localized access is the implementation of a many-to-many pivot relationship between the Account Managers and the Restaurants.
The architecture dictates the creation of a specialized pivot schema, designated as account_manager_restaurant. This table maps the user_id representing the Account Manager to the corresponding restaurant_id. Furthermore, it must include an assigned_by column pointing to the administrator who authorized the linkage, alongside standard timestamp columns to maintain an audit trail of the assignment itself.1 The system architecture must accommodate the possibility of overlapping assignments, such as one Account Manager overseeing digital marketing campaigns while a secondary Account Manager handles menu inventory and kitchen operations, although current operational constraints often restrict this to a single manager to maintain unblemished audit clarity.1
When an Account Manager authenticates via the Vue 3 Admin portal located at admin.khaalo.in, the backend system must aggressively and automatically intercept all Eloquent Object-Relational Mapping (ORM) queries. To ensure absolute data isolation and categorically prevent cross-contamination of restaurant data—a scenario that could lead to severe privacy breaches, misallocation of funds, or catastrophic unauthorized campaign launches—the multi-tenancy must be enforced globally at the ORM layer, rather than relying on developers to manually apply filters at the controller layer.10
Laravel's Global Scopes provide the optimal, most secure mechanism for this requirement.11 The engineering team must implement a custom TenantScope class that implements the Illuminate\Database\Eloquent\Scope interface. Within the apply method of this scope, the system evaluates the authenticated user's assigned role. If the system confirms the user holds the 'AccountManager' role, the scope automatically executes a subquery against the account_manager_restaurant pivot table to retrieve the array of authorized restaurant_ids. The scope subsequently appends a stringent WHERE IN SQL clause to every outbound database query across all relevant models (such as Ads, Offers, Orders, and Menus), dynamically filtering the records to only those associated with the permitted identifiers.11 This decoupled, abstract multi-tenancy core guarantees that malicious attempts, accidental API manipulations, or poorly written frontend queries cannot bypass the geographic or portfolio restrictions assigned to the Account Manager.12 When the Account Manager navigates to the Filament dashboard, the widgets, tables, and relationship managers will natively reflect only their assigned domain without requiring bespoke controller logic for every page.6
The Cryptographic State Machine for Campaigns
The central conflict resolved by this architectural blueprint is the secure delegation of campaign creation privileges to internal staff without conceding the ultimate activation authority. Account Managers must be empowered to leverage their expertise to configure complex marketing campaigns, but the restaurant partner bears the ultimate financial and operational responsibility for honoring those campaigns and paying for the associated discounts or advertising clicks.1 Therefore, the lifecycle of these entities must be tightly controlled by a cryptographic, multi-stage state machine.1
The approval workflow applies strictly to two primary domains managed within the isolated Catalog Service.1 The first domain comprises Advertisements (Ads), which include Pay-Per-Click (PPC) campaigns and featured Card Ads designed to utilize the restaurant's marketing budget to artificially boost their visibility in the Customer App's ranking algorithms. The second, more complex domain comprises Offers. The Offers module is multifaceted, encompassing direct Promo Codes defined by specific discount types (Flat Amount or Percentage), maximum financial caps, minimum purchase limits, scheduling windows (Start dates, End dates, precise Time of Day restrictions), and specific user targeting parameters.1 Furthermore, it includes Buy-One-Get-One (BOGO) discounts that require item-level mapping dictating buy quantities and free quantities, as well as Admin Opt-In Campaigns representing platform-wide sales events where the discount financial burden is split proportionally between the Khaalo platform and the restaurant.1
To govern the transition of these entities from creation to production visibility, both the ads and offers tables (along with their associated polymorphic variants) must be augmented with an approval_status enumerator. This field serves as the primary gating mechanism across the entire microservice ecosystem.1
The allowable states and their transition rules are defined precisely within the following structure:
State Designation
	Functional Description
	Transition Triggers and Preconditions
	Not Required
	The entity was created autonomously directly by the Restaurant owner via their Flutter application.
	Bypasses the approval flow entirely. The entity enters the standard Active or Scheduled lifecycle immediately upon database insertion.

	The foundational precondition rule governing this entire architecture dictates that an Account Manager-created advertisement or promotional offer cannot, under any circumstances, be consumed by any customer-facing API endpoint, rider application routing algorithm, or backend billing engine while its status remains Pending Restaurant Approval.1 In this state, it is effectively a "ghost" record, visible solely to the Account Manager who created it within their specific Filament dashboard, and surfaced to the Restaurant App's isolated Approvals queue for review.
The Real-Time Asynchronous Approval Pipeline
The lifecycle of an Account Manager-generated campaign represents a highly complex, fault-tolerant orchestration of front-end Vue 3 form validation, asynchronous microservice communication, push notification dispatch, real-time WebSocket event propagation, and cryptographic verification executing across the mobile Flutter client.1 This pipeline is executed in a precise, sequential manner.
Phase 1: Context Injection and Asynchronous Dispatch
The workflow initializes when an Account Manager successfully authenticates into the Vue 3 Admin Panel and navigates to the "Create Promo Code" or "Create Ad" Filament resource. Because the Account Manager is tightly scoped to specific restaurants via the Global Scope discussed previously, the initial step in the form creation requires selecting a target restaurant_id from a dynamically restricted dropdown menu.1 The Account Manager populates the campaign parameters, such as establishing a twenty percent discount with a maximum cap of one hundred rupees, requiring a minimum purchase of three hundred and ninety-nine rupees, and targeting exclusively new users.1
Upon the submission of this form, the Filament resource controller intercepts the standard Eloquent creation lifecycle. It programmatically injects contextual audit metadata into the payload before committing the transaction to the database.1 This injected payload explicitly defines the created_by_type as 'AccountManager', sets the created_by_id to the authenticated user's ID, and forces the approval_status to 'Pending Restaurant Approval', overriding any attempt by the client interface to bypass this state.1
The moment the MySQL transaction successfully commits within the Catalog Service, the architecture mandates the dispatch of a highly specific Laravel Event, designated as RequiresRestaurantApproval. This event serves as the catalyst for two immediate, asynchronous actions executed by the queued workers. First, a push notification payload is compiled and routed through the API Gateway, targeting the specific Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) device tokens associated with the restaurant owner's active sessions.1 Secondly, a real-time WebSocket event is emitted via the Laravel Reverb server on a heavily protected private channel specifically scoped to that restaurant (e.g., private-restaurant.{id}.approvals). If the restaurant owner is currently navigating the Flutter application, the internal state management instantly processes this WebSocket event, updating a numeric alert badge on the "Approvals" navigation tab without requiring any manual pull-to-refresh actions from the user.1
Phase 2: Mobile Interface and State Management
The Restaurant App, developed as a primary constituent of the Flutter monorepo, features a dedicated Approvals screen engineered to handle this specific workflow. The underlying architecture relies heavily on Riverpod's AsyncNotifier paradigms to handle the fetching and real-time updating of the pending entities list.4 By utilizing Riverpod, the application avoids the traditional, cumbersome setState anti-patterns, allowing the UI to react fluidly to background data mutations initiated by the WebSocket connections.4 The user interface strictly conforms to the overarching Khaalo design system: it employs an eight-pixel base unit grid, enforces a dark mode presentation utilizing a #0A0E1A background hex code, and standardizes typography using Space Grotesk for display headers and Inter for body text readability.1
When the user taps a pending item from the queue, a read-only detail view is presented, displaying the exact parameters submitted by the Account Manager.1 To maintain strict cryptographic auditability and simplify the state machine transitions, the initial system architecture enforces a rigid "Approve-as-submitted" or "Reject" paradigm.1 Direct editing of the pending fields by the restaurant owner prior to approval is expressly prohibited, as permitting such mutations would irrevocably fracture the audit trail detailing what the Account Manager originally proposed versus what was ultimately activated.1
Phase 3: OTP Generation, Delivery, and Cryptographic Security
When the restaurant owner initiates the authorization sequence by tapping the "Approve" button, a POST request is transmitted via the robust Dio HTTP client, routing from the Flutter application to the API Gateway.1 This initiates the critical OTP generation sequence. The backend controller generates a cryptographically secure, random six-digit numeric string.1
It is a paramount security directive that this OTP is never stored in plaintext within the database. The system immediately applies a cryptographic hashing function (utilizing SHA-256 or bcrypt) to the plain string. The resulting hash is stored in a highly specialized relational table named restaurant_approval_otps. This record associates the hash with the specific entity_type and entity_id of the pending campaign, logs the targeted restaurant_id, establishes an expires_at timestamp strictly configured to ten minutes from the exact moment of generation, and initializes an attempt_count integer at zero.1
Regarding the delivery mechanism, while the Khaalo platform possesses a fully integrated backend architecture for SMS delivery via Fast2SMS, current operational directives explicitly disable the transmission of SMS OTPs, favoring application-based or email-based delivery vectors.1 Consequently, the system leverages Laravel Mailables to compile a professionally formatted, responsive HTML email containing the plaintext six-digit code.15 This Mailable is pushed onto the Redis queue to prevent blocking the HTTP response thread, ensuring the Flutter application immediately transitions to the input phase while the SMTP servers dispatch the email to the primary address registered to the restaurant owner.1
Phase 4: Validation, Rate Limiting, and Activation
The Flutter application subsequently transitions to an OTP input dialog featuring a six-digit PIN entry interface and a prominent ten-minute countdown timer managed by a dedicated Riverpod provider. Upon the user entering the final digit, the Dio client automatically dispatches the verification payload to the backend.
At this juncture, stringent security and rate-limiting protocols are enforced. The introduction of an OTP gate is structurally compromised if the validation mechanism remains susceptible to brute-force traversal algorithms or denial-of-service attacks via OTP generation spamming.16 Therefore, Laravel's built-in RateLimiter facade is aggressively applied.17 The architecture enforces a strict generation throttle, permitting a restaurant to request a new OTP generation a maximum of three times within a fifteen-minute window to protect the email infrastructure.15 Furthermore, the validation endpoint strictly enforces a maximum of five incorrect entry attempts per OTP hash.1 The implementation must transactionally increment the attempt_count in the restaurant_approval_otps table upon every failed request.2 If the attempt_count reaches or exceeds five, the OTP record is permanently invalidated, returning an HTTP 422 Unprocessable Entity response to the Dio client, which Riverpod translates into a red error state and haptic vibration feedback for the user.2 To prevent distributed attacks, a Route::middleware('throttle:5,1') declaration binds the rate limit contemporaneously to the origin IP address and the restaurant_id.17
If the hashed input matches the stored hash and the timestamp precedes the expiration window, the validation succeeds. Because the approval of an entity requires disparate updates across multiple tables—updating the OTP record's verified_at timestamp, updating the core Ad/Offer status to Approved, logging the approved_at timestamp, setting approved_via to email_otp, and inserting a record into the account_manager_activity_logs—these operations are mandatorily wrapped within a DB::transaction() closure.1 If the Catalog Service fails to properly index the newly approved offer into the production database due to an intermittent failure, the Gateway Service automatically rolls back the entire OTP verification transaction, absolutely preventing the system from entering an inconsistent, orphaned state where the OTP is consumed but the campaign remains inactive. Following a successful commit, the Riverpod state invalidates the pending queue, smoothly removing the item from the user's view, and the campaign officially enters the active platform lifecycle.1

Strategic Technical Conclusions
The implementation of the Account Manager organizational role and its corresponding OTP-gated approval workflow fundamentally transforms the Khaalo platform from a basic digital ordering catalog into a financially secure, enterprise-grade multi-tenant operational ecosystem. By engineering and enforcing a strict cryptographic division of labor—wherein Account Managers possess unrestricted creative capacity to build complex campaigns but maintain absolute zero activation authority—the system architecture comprehensively mitigates the severe financial and reputational risks inherently associated with delegated marketing.
The utilization of Laravel's Global Scopes ensures that the multi-tenancy is hermetically sealed at the lowest database abstraction layer, protecting the platform against both malicious exploits and accidental data cross-contamination within the Vue 3 Administrative Panel. Concurrently, the deployment of an aggressively rate-limited, cryptographically hashed email OTP workflow operating within the high-performance Flutter mobile client ensures that the restaurant partner retains unassailable sovereign control over their public-facing promotions and subsequent financial liabilities. Furthermore, the mandatory, system-wide integration of the account_manager_activity_logs fortifies the ecosystem, providing an immutable ledger essential for future dispute resolution and granular operational audits. By adhering strictly to this comprehensive implementation blueprint, the engineering teams can rapidly deploy the highly requested Account Manager module while meticulously maintaining the rigorous performance, security, and architectural design standards foundational to the continued expansion of the Khaalo platform.
